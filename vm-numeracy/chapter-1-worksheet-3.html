Part A

Multiplying larger numbers

Slide 13

Worked example

Calculate 38 × 24

  1. Split 24 into 20 + 4
  2. 38 × 20 = 760
  3. 38 × 4 = 152
  4. Add the parts: 760 + 152 = 912

38 × 24 = 912

Tip: Split the second number into tens and ones, multiply each part, then add.

Part B

Multiplying money

Slide 14

Worked example

a) 6 coffees at $4.80 each    b) 2.5 kg of chicken at $12.40 per kg

  1. a) 6 × $4 = $24 and 6 × $0.80 = $4.80
  2. $24 + $4.80 = $28.80
  3. b) 2 × $12.40 = $24.80
  4. 0.5 × $12.40 = $6.20 (half), so $24.80 + $6.20 = $31.00

a) $28.80    b) $31.00

Tip: Split into easy parts: dollars and cents, or whole kilograms and half a kilogram.

Part C

Division with a remainder

Slide 15

Worked example

a) Rows hold 6 seats. How many rows do 158 people need?   b) How many full cartons of 6 can you fill with 158 eggs?

  1. 158 ÷ 6 = 26 remainder 2
  2. a) 26 rows leaves 2 people standing, so round up
  3. b) 2 eggs can’t fill a carton, so round down

a) 27 rows    b) 26 cartons

Tip: Ask: does the remainder still need something? Round up. Is it left over? Round down.

Part D

Multiplying and dividing negatives

Slide 16

Worked example

a) −6 × 4    b) −30 ÷ −5    c) 9 × −3

  1. Same signs give a positive answer. Different signs give a negative answer.
  2. a) different signs: 6 × 4 = 24, so −24
  3. b) same signs: 30 ÷ 5 = 6, so +6
  4. c) different signs: 9 × 3 = 27, so −27

a) −24    b) 6    c) −27

Tip: Work out the number first, then decide the sign.

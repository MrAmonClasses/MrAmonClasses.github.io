Part A

Percentages, fractions and decimals

Slide 23

Worked example

a) Write 35% as a fraction and a decimal   b) Write 34 as a percentage

  1. a) Per cent means out of 100: 35% = 35100 = 720
  2. 35 ÷ 100 = 0.35
  3. b) 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75
  4. 0.75 × 100 = 75%

a) 720 and 0.35    b) 75%

Tip: % → decimal: ÷ 100. Decimal → %: × 100.

Part B

The 10% method

Slide 24

Worked example

Find 35% of $240 without a calculator

  1. 10%: $240 ÷ 10 = $24
  2. 30%: 3 × $24 = $72
  3. 5%: half of 10% = $12
  4. 35% = 30% + 5% = $72 + $12 = $84

35% of $240 = $84

Tip: Find 10% first (÷ 10), then build up: 5% is half, 20% is double, 1% is ÷ 100.

Part C

Discounts

Slide 25

Worked example

A jacket costs $120. It is 35% off. What is the sale price?

  1. Method 1: discount = 0.35 × $120 = $42
  2. Sale price: $120 − $42 = $78
  3. Method 2: you pay 100% − 35% = 65%
  4. 0.65 × $120 = $78

The sale price is $78

Tip: Method 2 is faster on a calculator: % off → multiply by what you still pay.

Part D

Percentage increase and change

Slide 26

Worked example

a) $26.40 an hour rises by 4%. New rate?   b) Shifts go from 20 to 25 hours a week. % increase?

  1. a) New rate is 104% of $26.40: 1.04 × 26.40 = 27.456, round to $27.46
  2. b) % change = change ÷ original × 100
  3. Change = 25 − 20 = 5 hours
  4. 5 ÷ 20 × 100 = 25%

a) $27.46 an hour    b) 25% increase

Tip: For % change, always divide by the original (old) amount.

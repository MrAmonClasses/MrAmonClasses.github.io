We are learning to solve real problems using the problem-solving cycle and the order of operations.

Success criteria

  • I can use all four stages of the problem-solving cycle
  • I can find the maths in a real problem and estimate first

Section 1 · Solving problems

Using the problem-solving cycle

Worked example 3

Floor: 3 m × 2.4 m. Tiles: 30 cm × 30 cm, sold in boxes of 10 for $42. Add 10% extra. What will the tiles cost?

1 Identify the mathematics: tiles that fit, add 10%, round up to boxes, then cost. Use cm: 300 × 240.

2 Act on and use the mathematics: 300 ÷ 30 = 10 across and 240 ÷ 30 = 8 down

10 × 8 = 80 tiles

10% of 80 = 8 extra tiles

80 + 8 = 88 tiles

88 tiles needs 9 boxes (90 tiles)

9 × $42 = $378

3 Evaluate and reflect: estimate 9 boxes × $40 = $360. $378 is close, so it is reasonable.

4 Communicate and report: Buy 9 boxes of tiles for $378.

Click or press → for the next step

Buy 9 boxes for $378

Now you try 3

Laundry floor: 2.1 m × 1.8 m. Tiles: 30 cm × 30 cm, sold in boxes of 10 for $38. Add 10% extra. What will the tiles cost?

Answer: 210 ÷ 30 = 7, 180 ÷ 30 = 6, 7 × 6 = 42 tiles; + 10% = 46.2, so 47 tiles; 5 boxes (50 tiles); 5 × $38 = $190. Evaluate: estimate 5 × $40 = $200, so $190 is reasonable. Communicate: Buy 5 boxes for $190.

Use all four stages of the cycle.

Section 1 · Solving problems

Pay with overtime

Worked example 4

Sam works 38 hours at $24.50 an hour, plus 4 hours overtime at time and a half. What is Sam’s pay?

1 Identify the mathematics: normal pay + overtime pay. Overtime rate = $24.50 × 1.5.

2 Act on and use the mathematics: 38 × $24.50 = $931.00

$24.50 × 1.5 = $36.75 an hour

4 × $36.75 = $147.00

$931.00 + $147.00 = $1078.00

3 Evaluate and reflect: estimate 38 × $25 = $950 plus about 4 × $37 = $150, so about $1100. $1078 is close, so it is reasonable.

4 Communicate and report: Sam’s pay for the week is $1078.00.

Click or press → for the next step

Sam’s pay: $1078.00

Now you try 4

Jo works 35 hours at $22.80 an hour, plus 3 hours overtime at time and a half. What is Jo’s pay?

Answer: normal 35 × $22.80 = $798.00; overtime rate $22.80 × 1.5 = $34.20; 3 × $34.20 = $102.60; total $900.60. Evaluate: about 35 × $23 = $805 plus about 3 × $34 = $102, so roughly $900. Communicate: Jo’s pay is $900.60.

Use all four stages. Estimate first, then check your answer against it.

Section 1 · Solving Problems 1.06 · Activity 1C

Problem-solving cycle: worked example

Worked example · problem a

How much would a fish and chip dinner cost for your family?

1 Identify the mathematics: a family of 4. Fish, chips and a drink each. Find the total cost.

2 Act on and use the mathematics: estimate 4 × $15 = $60. Research: fish $9.50, a large chips $8, a drink $3 (a local shop’s menu). 4 × $9.50 = $38; 2 × $8 = $16; 4 × $3 = $12. Total $38 + $16 + $12 = $66.

3 Evaluate and reflect: $66 is close to the $60 estimate. Chips and drinks pushed it up a little, so it is reasonable.

4 Communicate and report: A fish and chip dinner for our family of 4 costs about $66.

Click or press → for the next step

Estimate $60, answer $66

Choose problem b, c, d or e from the book (Solving Problems 1.06). Use all four stages of the cycle and write a sentence at the end.

Check: did you estimate first, research real numbers (prices, speeds, hours), compare your answer to your estimate, and write a full-sentence answer?

Fraction of an amount

b) Calculate 3/4 × 2/5

a) Divide by the bottom: $360 ÷ 3 = $120

Section 4 · Fractions, decimals and ratios

Sharing in a ratio

Worked example 3

Concrete is mixed 1 cement : 2 sand : 3 gravel. How much of each is in a 60 kg mix?

Total parts: 1 + 2 + 3 = 6

One part: 60 kg ÷ 6 = 10 kg

Cement: 1 × 10 = 10 kg

Sand: 2 × 10 = 20 kg

Gravel: 3 × 10 = 30 kg

Check: 10 + 20 + 30 = 60 kg

Click or press → for the next step

10 kg cement, 20 kg sand, 30 kg gravel

Now you try 3

a) Mortar is mixed 1 cement : 4 sand. How much of each is in a 75 kg mix?

b) Cordial is mixed 1 cordial : 4 water. How much of each goes in a 2 L (2000 mL) jug?

Answers: a) 5 parts, one part = 15 kg: 15 kg cement, 60 kg sand. b) 5 parts, one part = 400 mL: 400 mL cordial, 1600 mL water.

Add the parts, find one part, then multiply.

Section 4 · Fractions, decimals and ratios

Rates

Worked example 4

a) You drive 270 km in 3 hours. What is your average speed?

b) You earn $186 for 7.5 hours. What is your hourly rate?

Rate = amount ÷ time

a) 270 ÷ 3 = 90

b) 186 ÷ 7.5 = 24.80

Click or press → for the next step

a) 90 km/h    b) $24.80 per hour

Now you try 4

a) You drive 156 km in 2 hours. What is your average speed?

b) You earn $243.60 for 8.4 hours. What is your hourly rate?

c) A car uses 45 L of fuel to travel 600 km. How many litres per 100 km?

Answers: a) 156 ÷ 2 = 78 km/h. b) 243.60 ÷ 8.4 = $29.00 per hour. c) 600 km is 6 lots of 100 km, so 45 ÷ 6 = 7.5 L per 100 km.

Always write the units with your answer.

The 10% method

Worked example 2

Find 35% of $240 without a calculator

10%: $240 ÷ 10 = $24

30%: 3 × $24 = $72

5%: half of 10% = $12

35% = 30% + 5% = $72 + $12

Click or press → for the next step

= $84

Now you try 2

No calculator. Find:

a) 15% of $180

b) 40% of $65

c) 25% of $96

Answers: a) 10% = $18, 5% = $9, total $27. b) 10% = $6.50, × 4 = $26. c) 10% = $9.60, 20% = $19.20, 5% = $4.80, total $24 (or a quarter of $96).

Find 10% first, then build up.

Section 5 · Percentages

Percentage increase and change

Worked example 4

a) $26.40 an hour rises by 4%. New rate?

b) Shifts go from 20 to 25 hours a week. % increase?

a) 104% of $26.40: 1.04 × 26.40 = 27.456

b) % change = change ÷ original × 100

Change: 25 − 20 = 5 hours

5 ÷ 20 = 0.25

0.25 × 100 = 25%

Click or press → for the next step

Now you try 4

b) A price drops from $80 to $68. % decrease?

Section 6 · Rounding, powers and roots

Pythagoras’ theorem

Worked example 4

A frame is 1.2 m wide and 0.9 m high. How long is the diagonal brace?

Longest side: c² = a² + b²

c² = 1.2² + 0.9²

c² = 1.44 + 0.81

c² = 2.25

c = √2.25

Click or press → for the next step

c = 1.5 m

Now you try 4

a) A gate is 1.6 m wide and 1.2 m high. How long is its diagonal brace?

b) A ramp rises 0.5 m over a 4 m run. How long is the ramp, to 2 decimal places?

Answers: a) 1.6² + 1.2² = 2.56 + 1.44 = 4, √4 = 2 m. b) 4² + 0.5² = 16 + 0.25 = 16.25, √16.25 = 4.031… → 4.03 m.

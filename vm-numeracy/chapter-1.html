Section 1 · Coursebook: 4-Stage Problem-Solving Cycle

The 4-stage problem-solving cycle

1. Identify the maths

Find, identify and interpret the numerical information. Look for:

numbers · words · images · quantities · symbols · measures · dimensions · shapes · sizes · directions · angles · times · patterns · sequences · ratios · questions · problems · data · proportions · formulae

2. Act on and use maths

Do the estimates, calculations or actions, and use suitable technologies. Such as:

estimating · measuring · calculating · comparing · analysing · solving · making · sketching and drawing · designing · rendering · constructing · building

4. Communicate & report

Share results and findings using a range of methods and media. Consider:

selecting · explaining · describing · summarising · graphing · evaluating · words · numbers · format · method · media · technologies

3. Evaluate and reflect

Check that the right information was used and the right maths was done.

Did I perform the appropriate steps? · Does my answer seem correct? · Is the result close to my estimate? · How can I double-check? · Did I apply the correct tools? · What did I do well? · What do I need to improve?

Using the problem-solving cycle

Worked example 3

Floor: 3 m × 2.4 m. Tiles: 30 cm × 30 cm, sold in boxes of 10 for $42. Add 10% extra. What will the tiles cost?

1 Identify the maths:
  • Find: boxes needed and the total cost
  • Floor 300 cm × 240 cm (change m to cm)
  • Tile 30 cm × 30 cm, add 10% extra
  • Round up to whole boxes: 10 tiles for $42

2 Act on and use maths: Estimate with easy numbers: about 90 tiles is 9 boxes, and 9 × $40 = $360

300 ÷ 30 = 10 tiles across

240 ÷ 30 = 8 tiles down

10 × 8 = 80 tiles

10% of 80 = 8 extra tiles

80 + 8 = 88 tiles

88 tiles needs 9 boxes (90 tiles)

9 × $42 = $378

3 Evaluate and reflect:
  • Is the result close to my estimate? $378 is a little over $360 because $42 is more than $40
  • Does my answer seem correct? 90 tiles covers the 88 needed

4 Communicate & report: Buy 9 boxes of tiles for $378.

Click or press → for the next step

Buy 9 boxes for $378

Now you try 3

Laundry floor: 2.1 m × 1.8 m. Tiles: 30 cm × 30 cm, sold in boxes of 10 for $38. Add 10% extra. What will the tiles cost?

Answer: 210 ÷ 30 = 7, 180 ÷ 30 = 6, 7 × 6 = 42 tiles; + 10% = 46.2, so 47 tiles; 5 boxes (50 tiles); 5 × $38 = $190. Act: estimate 5 × $40 = $200. Evaluate: $190 is close to $200. Communicate: Buy 5 boxes for $190.

Pay with overtime

Worked example 4

Sam works 38 hours at $24.50 an hour, plus 4 hours overtime at time and a half. What is Sam’s pay?

1 Identify the maths:
  • Find: Sam’s total pay
  • Normal pay: 38 hours × $24.50
  • Overtime: 4 hours at time and a half (× 1.5)
  • Total = normal pay + overtime pay

2 Act on and use maths: Estimate with easy numbers: 40 × $25 = $1000, overtime about 4 × $40 = $160, so about $1160

38 × $24.50 = $931.00

$24.50 × 1.5 = $36.75 an hour

4 × $36.75 = $147.00

$931.00 + $147.00 = $1078.00

3 Evaluate and reflect:
  • Is the result close to my estimate? $1078 is a bit under $1160, which makes sense: I rounded every number up
  • How can I double-check? Redo 38 × $24.50 on the calculator

4 Communicate & report: Sam’s pay for the week is $1078.00.

Click or press → for the next step

Sam’s pay: $1078.00

Now you try 4

Jo works 35 hours at $22.80 an hour, plus 3 hours overtime at time and a half. What is Jo’s pay?

Answer: normal 35 × $22.80 = $798.00; overtime rate $22.80 × 1.5 = $34.20; 3 × $34.20 = $102.60; total $900.60. Act: estimate 35 × $23 = $805 plus about 3 × $34 = $102, so about $900. Evaluate: $900.60 is close. Communicate: Jo’s pay is $900.60.

Pythagoras’ theorem

Worked example 4

A frame is 1.2 m wide and 0.9 m high. How long is the diagonal brace?

1 Identify the maths:
  • Find: the brace, the longest side c
  • Right-angled triangle: a = 1.2 m, b = 0.9 m
  • Pythagoras: c² = a² + b²

2 Act on and use maths: Estimate: c is longer than 1.2 m but shorter than 1.2 + 0.9 = 2.1 m

c² = 1.2² + 0.9²

c² = 1.44 + 0.81

c² = 2.25

c = √2.25

c = 1.5

3 Evaluate and reflect:
  • Is the result close to my estimate? 1.5 m is between 1.2 m and 2.1 m
  • How can I double-check? 1.5² = 2.25

4 Communicate & report: The diagonal brace is 1.5 m long.

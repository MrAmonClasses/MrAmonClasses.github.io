Part A

Order of operations: × and ÷ first

Slide 3

Worked example

Calculate 18 + 6 × 4 − 10 ÷ 2

  1. 1 Identify the maths:
    • Find: the value of the expression
    • Operations: +, ×, − and ÷
    • Order: × and ÷ first, then + and − from left to right
  2. 2 Act on and use maths: 18 + 6 × 4 − 10 ÷ 2
  3. = 18 + 24 − 10 ÷ 2
  4. = 18 + 24 − 5
  5. = 42 − 5
  6. = 37
  7. 3 Evaluate and reflect:
    • Did I perform the appropriate steps? × and ÷ came before + and −

4 Communicate & report: 18 + 6 × 4 − 10 ÷ 2 = 37.

Tip: × and ÷ come before + and −. When there are only × and ÷ (or only + and −), work left to right.

Part B

Order of operations: brackets first

Slide 4

Worked example

Calculate (15 − 7) × 4 + 6

  1. 1 Identify the maths:
    • Find: the value of the expression
    • Brackets first
    • Then ×, then +
  2. 2 Act on and use maths: (15 − 7) × 4 + 6
  3. = 8 × 4 + 6
  4. = 32 + 6
  5. = 38
  6. 3 Evaluate and reflect:
    • Did I perform the appropriate steps? Brackets came first

4 Communicate & report: (15 − 7) × 4 + 6 = 38.

Tip: Order: Brackets, then × and ÷ (left to right), then + and − (left to right).

Part C

Using the problem-solving cycle

Slide 6

Worked example

A floor is 3 m × 2.4 m. Tiles are 30 cm × 30 cm, sold in boxes of 10 for $42. Add 10% extra for cuts and breakages. What will the tiles cost?

  1. 1 Identify the maths:
    • Find: boxes needed and the total cost
    • Floor 300 cm × 240 cm (change m to cm)
    • Tile 30 cm × 30 cm, add 10% extra
    • Round up to whole boxes: 10 tiles for $42
  2. 2 Act on and use maths: Estimate with easy numbers: about 90 tiles is 9 boxes, and 9 × $40 = $360
  3. 300 ÷ 30 = 10 tiles across
  4. 240 ÷ 30 = 8 tiles down
  5. 10 × 8 = 80 tiles
  6. 10% of 80 = 8 extra tiles
  7. 80 + 8 = 88 tiles
  8. 88 tiles needs 9 boxes (90 tiles)
  9. 9 × $42 = $378
  10. 3 Evaluate and reflect:
    • Is the result close to my estimate? $378 is a little over $360 because $42 is more than $40
    • Does my answer seem correct? 90 tiles covers the 88 needed

4 Communicate & report: Buy 9 boxes of tiles for $378.

Tip: Use all four stages of the problem-solving cycle: Identify the maths, Act on and use maths (this is where you estimate and calculate), Evaluate and reflect (check against your estimate), Communicate & report. Finish with a sentence.

Part D

Pay with overtime

Slide 7

Worked example

Sam works 38 hours at $24.50 an hour, plus 4 hours overtime at time and a half. What is Sam’s pay?

  1. 1 Identify the maths:
    • Find: Sam’s total pay
    • Normal pay: 38 hours × $24.50
    • Overtime: 4 hours at time and a half (× 1.5)
    • Total = normal pay + overtime pay
  2. 2 Act on and use maths: Estimate with easy numbers: 40 × $25 = $1000, overtime about 4 × $40 = $160, so about $1160
  3. 38 × $24.50 = $931.00
  4. $24.50 × 1.5 = $36.75 an hour
  5. 4 × $36.75 = $147.00
  6. $931.00 + $147.00 = $1078.00
  7. 3 Evaluate and reflect:
    • Is the result close to my estimate? $1078 is a bit under $1160, which makes sense: I rounded every number up
    • How can I double-check? Redo 38 × $24.50 on the calculator

4 Communicate & report: Sam’s pay for the week is $1078.00.

Tip: Time and a half = × 1.5. Double time = × 2. Find the overtime rate first. Use all four stages: Identify the maths, Act on and use maths (estimate, then calculate), Evaluate and reflect (is it close to your estimate?), Communicate & report (a sentence).

Part E

Problem-solving cycle practice: Solving Problems 1.06

Slide 8

Worked example

a) How much would a fish and chip dinner cost for your family? (Example prices: fish $9.50, large chips $8, drink $3. Use real prices you research.)

  1. 1 Identify the maths:
    • Find: total cost for a family of 4
    • Fish, chips and a drink for each person
    • Research real prices
  2. 2 Act on and use maths: Estimate with easy numbers: 4 × $15 = $60
  3. Research: fish $9.50, large chips $8, drink $3
  4. 4 × $9.50 = $38
  5. 2 × $8 = $16
  6. 4 × $3 = $12
  7. $38 + $16 = $54
  8. $54 + $12 = $66
  9. 3 Evaluate and reflect:
    • Is the result close to my estimate? $66 is close to $60
    • Does my answer seem correct? Everyone has fish and a drink
    • What did I do well? What do I need to improve?

4 Communicate & report: A fish and chip dinner for our family of 4 costs about $66.

Tip: Use the four stages: Identify the maths (dot points: what to find, what you know, the maths to use), Act on and use maths (estimate, research, calculate), Evaluate and reflect (is it close to your estimate? does it seem correct? what did you do well? what can you improve?), Communicate & report (a full sentence).

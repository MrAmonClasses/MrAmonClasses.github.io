Part A

Adding and subtracting decimals

Slide 8

Worked example

Calculate 12.5 + 3.75 + 108.2

  1. Set it out in columns with the decimal points lined up
  2. Fill the gaps with zeros: 12.50 + 3.75 + 108.20
  3. Add the columns, starting from the right

12.5 + 3.75 + 108.2 = 124.45

Tip: Line up the decimal points, not the last digits. Estimate first: 13 + 4 + 108 = 125.

Part B

Money and change

Slide 9

Worked example

You buy items for $14.95, $6.40 and $23.50, and pay with $50. How much change do you get?

  1. Estimate: $15 + $6 + $24 = $45, so about $5 change
  2. Total: $14.95 + $6.40 + $23.50 = $44.85
  3. Change: $50.00 − $44.85 = $5.15
  4. Check: $5.15 is close to the $5 estimate

Change is $5.15

Tip: Estimate first, then check your answer is close.

Part C

Adding fractions

Slide 10

Worked example

a) 38 + 78    b) 2 14 + 1 34

  1. a) Same bottom number, so add the tops: 3 + 7 = 10, giving 108
  2. 108 = 1 28 = 1 14
  3. b) Add the wholes: 2 + 1 = 3
  4. Add the parts: 14 + 34 = 44 = 1, so 3 + 1 = 4

a) 1 14    b) 4

Tip: When the bottom numbers (denominators) are the same, add or subtract the tops. Simplify at the end.

Part D

Negative numbers

Slide 11

Worked example

a) At 6 am it is −4 °C. By noon it is 11 degrees warmer. What is the temperature?   b) Your bank balance is −$35. You deposit $120, then spend $64. What is your balance?

  1. a) Start at −4 and go up 11 on a number line: −4 + 11 = 7
  2. b) −35 + 120 = 85
  3. Then 85 − 64 = 21

a) 7 °C    b) $21

Tip: Adding moves you up the number line. Subtracting moves you down. Draw a number line if it helps.

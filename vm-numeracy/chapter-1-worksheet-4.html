Part A

Fractions to decimals and back

Slide 21

Worked example

a) 34   b) 75 as decimals    c) 0.35 as a fraction

  1. 1 Identify the maths:
    • Find: a) and b) as decimals, c) as a fraction
    • Fraction to decimal: divide top by bottom
    • Decimal to fraction: use place value, then simplify
  2. 2 Act on and use maths: Estimate: a) between 0.5 and 1 b) a bit more than 1 c) a bit over a third
  3. a) 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75
  4. b) 7 ÷ 5 = 1.4
  5. c) 0.35 = 35100
  6. 35100 = 720 (÷ 5 top and bottom)
  7. 3 Evaluate and reflect:
    • Is the result close to my estimate? Yes, all three
    • How can I double-check? 7 ÷ 20 = 0.35

4 Communicate & report: a) 34 = 0.75 b) 75 = 1.4 c) 0.35 = 720.

Tip: One decimal place = tenths, two = hundredths, three = thousandths.

Part B

Fraction of an amount

Slide 22

Worked example

a) You spend 23 of your $360 pay on rent. How much is rent?   b) Calculate 34 × 25

  1. 1 Identify the maths:
    • Find: a) rent b) the product
    • a) fraction of an amount: divide by the bottom, multiply by the top
    • b) multiply tops, multiply bottoms, then simplify
  2. 2 Act on and use maths: Estimate: a) 23 is more than half, so more than $180 b) under 12
  3. a) $360 ÷ 3 = $120
  4. $120 × 2 = $240
  5. b) 3 × 2 = 6 (tops)
  6. 4 × 5 = 20 (bottoms)
  7. 620 = 310 (÷ 2 top and bottom)
  8. 3 Evaluate and reflect:
    • Is the result close to my estimate? a) $240 is more than $180 b) 310 is under 12

4 Communicate & report: a) Rent is $240. b) 34 × 25 = 310.

Tip: Fraction of an amount: divide by the bottom, multiply by the top.

Part C

Sharing in a ratio

Slide 23

Worked example

Concrete is mixed 1 cement : 2 sand : 3 gravel. How much of each is in a 60 kg mix?

  1. 1 Identify the maths:
    • Find: kg of cement, sand and gravel
    • Ratio 1 : 2 : 3 in a 60 kg mix
    • Total parts, then the size of one part
  2. 2 Act on and use maths: Estimate: gravel is 3 of the 6 parts, so half of 60 kg = 30 kg
  3. Total parts: 1 + 2 + 3 = 6
  4. One part: 60 kg ÷ 6 = 10 kg
  5. Cement: 1 × 10 = 10 kg
  6. Sand: 2 × 10 = 20 kg
  7. Gravel: 3 × 10 = 30 kg
  8. 3 Evaluate and reflect:
    • Is the result close to my estimate? Gravel is 30 kg
    • How can I double-check? 10 + 20 + 30 = 60 kg

4 Communicate & report: The mix has 10 kg cement, 20 kg sand and 30 kg gravel.

Tip: Add the parts, find one part, then multiply.

Part D

Rates

Slide 24

Worked example

a) You drive 270 km in 3 hours. What is your average speed?   b) You earn $186 for 7.5 hours. What is your hourly rate?

  1. 1 Identify the maths:
    • Find: a) speed in km/h b) pay per hour
    • Rate = amount ÷ time
  2. 2 Act on and use maths: Estimate with easy numbers: a) 300 ÷ 3 = 100 b) $200 ÷ 8 = $25
  3. a) 270 ÷ 3 = 90
  4. b) 186 ÷ 7.5 = 24.80
  5. 3 Evaluate and reflect:
    • Is the result close to my estimate? a) 90 is a bit under 100: I rounded up b) $24.80 is close to $25

4 Communicate & report: a) The average speed is 90 km/h. b) The rate is $24.80 per hour.

Tip: Always write the units: km/h, $/h, L/min, $/kg.

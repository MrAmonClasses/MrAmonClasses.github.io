Section 1 · The three outcomes

How you pass Unit 3

Each unit is marked S (Satisfactory) or N (Not Satisfactory). To get an S you must show all three outcomes. Your worksheets, assessment tasks and projects are the evidence.

Outcome 1

Numeracy in context

Find the maths in real-life situations, use it, and judge whether your answers make sense.

Outcome 2

The problem-solving cycle

Plan, solve, check and report on real problems using the four stages of the cycle.

Outcome 3

Mathematical toolkit

Choose and use the right tools accurately: calculators, tape measures, spreadsheets and apps.

Section 1 · The three outcomes

Outcome 1: Numeracy in context

Extract, evaluate and apply the maths from Areas of Study 1–4, in routine, non-routine, unfamiliar and some specialised contexts, across the chosen numeracies.

VCE VM Numeracy study design, Unit 3 Outcome 1 (shortened)

In plain words: find the maths hiding in a real situation, use it, and decide if your answer is reasonable. The situations come from the numeracies (Section 2). The maths comes from the areas of study (Section 3).

RoutineTasks people do often, like working out change or a shift’s pay.
UnfamiliarSituations you haven’t met before, so you must work out which maths fits.
SpecialisedTasks with their own words and rules, like a trade’s mixing ratios or tolerances.

Section 1 · The three outcomes

Outcome 2: The problem-solving cycle

1 Identify: What is the question? What information matters? Make a plan and an estimate.

2 Act: Do the calculations and measurements, by hand, in your head or with technology.

3 Evaluate: Compare with your estimate. Does it make sense in real life? Fix it if not.

4 Communicate: Show your working and give the answer in a sentence, with units.

Section 1 · The three outcomes

Outcome 3: Your mathematical toolkit

Choose the right tool for the job, use it accurately, and check what it tells you.

Hands-on tools

  • Tape measures and rulers
  • Kitchen scales and jugs
  • Clocks and calendars
  • Tools of your trade

Digital tools

  • Calculators
  • Spreadsheets
  • Measuring, level and angle apps
  • Online calculators and planners

What you must do

  • Pick the right tool and settings
  • Compare results with your estimate
  • Spot errors and accuracy limits
  • Stay safe online

Section 3 · Area of Study 1 · Chapter 1

Area of Study 1: Number

By the end you will be able to

  • Read very large and very small numbers
  • Calculate with positive and negative numbers
  • Round whole numbers, and decimals to 3 places
  • Use the order of operations
  • Move between fractions, decimals and percentages
  • Calculate percentage increase and decrease
  • Use powers (up to cubed) and square roots
  • Solve problems with ratios and proportions

Section 3 · Area of Study 2 · Chapter 2

Area of Study 2: Shape

By the end you will be able to

  • Name and sort 2D shapes and 3D objects, like cones and pyramids
  • Find lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry
  • Use angle facts: 180° in a triangle, 360° in a quadrilateral
  • Tell when shapes are congruent or similar
  • Build compound shapes and objects, including with technology
  • Enlarge and reduce shapes using a scale
  • Spot shape patterns in bridges, buildings and design

Section 3 · Area of Study 3 · Chapters 3 and 4

Area of Study 3: Quantity and measures

By the end you will be able to

  • Estimate and measure with the right accuracy and tolerance
  • Find perimeter, area, volume and capacity, including compound shapes
  • Use π for circles
  • Convert metric units, and metric to non-metric (cm/inch, °C/°F, g/lb)
  • Read 24-hour time and work across time zones
  • Calculate durations using calendars and clocks
  • Read and calculate with temperatures

Section 3 · Area of Study 4 · Chapter 5

Area of Study 4: Relationships

By the end you will be able to

  • Use rates such as km/h, L/h, $/m³ and wages per hour
  • Use ratios and scales on maps and plans
  • Use formulas by substituting values
  • Show a relationship in a table, graph or formula
  • Describe how one quantity changes with another
  • Use proportions when mixing chemicals or ingredients

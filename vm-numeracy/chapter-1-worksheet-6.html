Part A

Rounding to decimal places

Slide 28

Worked example

Round 47.386 to a) 1 decimal place b) 2 decimal places c) the nearest whole number

  1. Look at the next digit: 5 or more, round up. 4 or less, leave it.
  2. a) 47.386: next digit 8, round up → 47.4
  3. b) 47.386: next digit 6, round up → 47.39
  4. c) 47.386: next digit 3, leave it → 47

a) 47.4    b) 47.39    c) 47

Tip: Underline the digit after the place you need. That digit decides.

Part B

Cash rounding to 5 cents

Slide 29

Worked example

You pay cash. Round: a) $12.62   b) $12.63   c) $12.67   d) $12.68

  1. Ends in 1 or 2: round down to 0
  2. Ends in 3 or 4: round up to 5
  3. Ends in 6 or 7: round down to 5
  4. Ends in 8 or 9: round up to 10

a) $12.60   b) $12.65   c) $12.65   d) $12.70

Tip: Only cash totals are rounded. Card payments are charged to the exact cent.

Part C

Powers and square roots

Slide 30

Worked example

a) 6²    b) 4³    c) √81    d) √50 to 2 decimal places

  1. a) 6² = 6 × 6 = 36
  2. b) 4³ = 4 × 4 × 4 = 64
  3. c) Which number times itself is 81? 9 × 9 = 81, so √81 = 9
  4. d) Calculator: √50 = 7.071… → 7.07

a) 36   b) 64   c) 9   d) 7.07

Tip: Squared means × itself. Cubed means × itself three times. Square root undoes squaring.

Part D

Pythagoras’ theorem

Slide 31

Worked example

A frame is 1.2 m wide and 0.9 m high. How long is the diagonal brace?

  1. The brace is the longest side: c² = a² + b²
  2. c² = 1.2² + 0.9² = 1.44 + 0.81
  3. c² = 2.25, so c = √2.25
  4. c = 1.5

The brace is 1.5 m long

Tip: Sketch the right-angled triangle and label the sides first. The longest side is opposite the right angle.
